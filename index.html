2026 Season

BioGlow

🏆 1st Championship — Qualifiers ✓ Qualified to State Championship

Innovation Project: VMD Thermosolar Hives

Problem: Over 60% of honeybees are affected by varroa mites—the #1 cause of honeybee deaths—killing ~50% of affected bees (~1.65 million colonies / ~49.5 billion bees dying in the U.S. each season) and threatening pollination (-90% pollination rate) and food supply (-20%).

Solution: The VMD Thermosolar Hive is a non-chemical, transferable smart-hive system powered by solar panels (with USB-C backup). Internal AI camera sensors, motion sensors, and thermal sensors monitor bees for mites and trigger a bottom heater to raise the hive temperature to 104°F—deadly to varroa mites while safe for bees, honey, and wax—dropping dead mites into a bottom collecting tray.

Specialist Research: Consulted 8 specialists:

  • Ms. Ana Heck — Apiculture Extension Educator at Michigan State University (MSU) who educates beekeepers, growers, pesticide applicators, and the public on honey bee health and pollinator protection.
  • Prof. Grace McCormack — Zoologist and Personal Professor at the University of Galway who leads research on the dark native Irish honey bee and free-living honeybee colonies.
  • Mr. Jerry Hayes — Beekeeping expert and Bee Culture magazine editor.
  • Mr. Stephen Martin — UK entomologist and Chair in Social Entomology at the University of Salford, known for nearly four decades of research on social insects, honeybee pests, and Varroa destructor mites.
  • Ms. Melanie Kirby — Cofounder of Zia Queenbees Farm; has researched bees and pollination for over 3 decades.
  • Mr. Egbert N. Maben — A beekeeper for 35+ years.
  • Mr. Randy Oliver — Prominent American commercial beekeeper, biologist, researcher, and educator based in Northern California, best known as the founder of Scientific Beekeeping.
  • Mr. Paul Ternlund — Beekeeper and owner of an apiary in Aurora, CO.

Robot Design & Strategy

  • Chassis & Balance: Rectangular frame with upward-facing front motors (for fast attachment swaps), rear hub for balanced weight, and large smooth wheels.
  • Sensors & Code: Programmed in SPIKE Prime word blocks using a gyro sensor for speed-calibrated turns and automatic obstacle re-centering.
  • Attachments & Runs: Combined passive and motorized attachments (including combining Missions 2, 4, 5, 8, and 9 into one run, plus a slide-under passive attachment for Mission 9 Research Platform) with built-in failsafes and testing logs.
2025 Season

Unearthed

🏆 1st Place Robot Performance — Qualifiers 🏆 1st Place Robot Design — Qualifiers 🥈 2nd Place Innovation — State Championship 🌍 Top 6 · Open Invitational International Tournaments

Innovation Project: ArcheoConnect & Godzilla 2.0 Rover

Problem: Analyzing an archaeological artifact typically takes at least 3 weeks (and longer in hard-to-reach mountains, deserts, or caves), with ~90% of found artifacts already known.

Solution: Built ArcheoConnect with Replit AI—featuring global artifact search, live AI camera analysis ("Auto Click" identifying artifacts in seconds, 3,600× faster and saving 75–90% of analysis time), multilingual real-time translated group chats with an AI gatekeeper, and "Submit Discovery" alerts. Paired with Godzilla 2.0, an all-terrain rover with smartphone live camera feed, lights, sensors, claws, and GPS alerts.

Specialist Research: Consulted Dr. Holly Norton (Colorado State Archaeologist, History Colorado) and Mr. Scott Ortman (Director, Center for Collaborative Synthesis in Archaeology, CU Boulder), plus visited the Denver Historical Museum.

Robot Design & Strategy

  • Bot & Code: Compact 5" × 6" × 3½" (4.1" height) box-shaped robot with front motors and rear hub/wheels. Upgraded from word blocks to Pybricks (Python) with use_gyro=True, async(), and testing logs targeting >90% run consistency.
  • Improvements: Replaced slipping gears with direct motors; built 8 reusable/pinless attachments (including 2 multipurpose attachments completing 8 missions) and failsafe runs across 15 table missions.

2024 Season

Submerged

🏆 1st Place Robot Design — Qualifiers 🤝 Peer Award — Qualifiers ⭐ Best Coach Award — State Championship ✓ Qualified to State Championship

Innovation Project: AMR Godzilla (Ghost Net Retrieval)

Problem: Lost fishing gear ("ghost nets") harm marine life and scuba divers—killing ~650,000 marine animals annually, with 500,000–600,000 tons abandoned each year and only ~1% (~300 nets) recovered.

Solution: Designed AMR (Autonomous Marine Robot) – Godzilla, a 3D-printed autonomous marine vehicle with interior sonar-based scanning and a shredder + barrel container, plus exterior 6–8 claws, an airlock, and an upgraded camera and light for detection.

Specialist Research: Consulted Dr. Sandhya K.M. (Senior Scientist, Fishing Technology) and Prof. Arthur Trembanis (Professor of Marine Science and Policy, University of Delaware).

Robot Design & Mission Runs

  • Attachment Efficiency: Combined motorized attachments (for complex missions) and passive attachments (freeing up motorized ports in the same run), reusable across multiple missions to save pitstop time.
  • Mission Runs (Runs 0–6): Structured by mission difficulty and distance from home area—covering Seabed Sample, Unexpected Encounter, Plankton & Water Sample, Mission 15 Drop, and Shark Drop.
